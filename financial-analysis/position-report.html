What you owe before you spend anything

 estimates · verify before filing

Source deductions and GST are trust funds, and you are personally liable

You confirmed no GST and no payroll remittances have been made this year. Both are money collected on someone else's behalf. Penalties on unremitted source deductions run at 10% for a first failure and 20% for a repeat, plus interest. Directors are personally liable for both — incorporating does not shield you from these two debts specifically. Ask your accountant about the Voluntary Disclosures Program before filing; approaching CRA first generally produces a materially better outcome than being found.

Where this leaves you

 the short version

Your total liquid position is $135,018. $67,159 in the sole proprietor's EQ account, $32,108 in the corporation's, $17,851 in TD chequing and $17,900 at Wealthsimple. Strip out the investments, whose accessibility depends on how the account is registered, and you have $117,118 in true cash.

The business generated $99,267 of net cash from a zero start, after paying you $33,387. In cash terms the first season was a success.

Roughly $45,548 of that is not yours. Unremitted GST, unremitted payroll deductions with penalty, and the personal tax on sole-proprietorship profit. Open a separate savings account and move it out of the operating account this week — money that sits in a chequing account gets spent.

Deployable business capital is about $11,058. That funds an equipment budget or a financed vehicle down payment. It does not fund a cash vehicle purchase, and it does not fund a second hire in the same year as a vehicle. Your personal $17,851 is not a second source of capital here — a large share of it is spoken for by the same personal tax bill.

The biggest uses of cash were you and your credit cards. $33,387 in owner draws and $35,997 in card payments together account for $69,384 — more than double what went to suppliers. The card spending is the single largest thing in this report that nobody can currently explain.

Two structural problems need your accountant before January 31. The corporation traded for two months out of the sole proprietor's bank account, and your $4,000 monthly draw is an undeclared shareholder loan. Both are fixable now and expensive later.
